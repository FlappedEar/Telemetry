## FlappedEar Telemetry 0.2.27 (pre-release)

A new **Best phases** card under the theoretical best builds a target from the day's best corner parts: the best entry of one lap, the best middle of another, the best exit of a third. It installs over 0.2.26 and earlier versions and over Android builds from `main`, and keeps your saved days. From 0.2.6 on, **Settings › Check for updates** finds this release.

### Best phases

- The card is closed at first, with one line that gives the total once it is worked out; tap **Show** to open it.
- Each corner is split where it is tightest into **entry**, **middle** and **exit**, the same split as in the corner details, so every lap is timed over the same metres. Each part comes from the ranked lap that was fastest through it. A straight, and a corner that is not split, comes whole from the lap fastest through it, and a corner that is not split says why, for example *Not split: more than one tight part* or *Not split: crosses the start/finish line*. Laps that are not ranked are left out.
- At the top you see the parts added up, and next to it the chosen lap's time over the same segments and what it loses. **Part by part** lists each part with its best time and lap.
- **Against the theoretical bests** puts the total beside the Theoretical best card's totals. **Fastest segments** is the same idea a whole segment at a time. Best phases is never slower than it, because a corner's three parts can come from three laps; the card says so only when its totals show it. **Best phases that join** applies the rule of segments that join to the parts: wherever a part from one lap meets a part from another, the speeds must differ by at most 2 km/h. **Best typical** is a different idea, what you did through each segment as a rule, and it is not split.
- **Where laps meet** says at how many lines two laps meet and whether their speeds match there. A matching speed is needed for two laps to join, but it is not proof that they do.
- A part's speed without a unit is read as km/h, and the card says so. A speed with a declared unit keeps that unit; the card never assumes km/h for it.
