## FlappedEar Telemetry 0.2.19 (pre-release)

The session summary on **Coach** can now open every segment's change since the session before, not only the biggest gain and loss. Corner details now split a corner's time into entry, middle and exit against the best lap, and a new map shows where the laps vary. A brake or throttle recorded as 0 to 1 with no unit is now read as a fraction when the longitudinal G shows it. It installs over 0.2.18 and earlier versions and over Android builds from `main`, and keeps your saved days. From 0.2.6 on, **Settings › Check for updates** finds this release.

### Every segment since the session before

- Under **Biggest gain** and **Biggest loss** on the session summary, a new button such as **All 13 segments against Session 5** opens a page with every segment the summary compared. It appears once the segments are timed and there is a session before to compare with.
- The page shows the lap-time spread of both sessions, then the segments under **Quicker** and **Slower**, each with the biggest change first, and last the segments that changed by less than 0.05 s, under **Within 0.05 s of Session 5**. Each segment shows its typical (median) time in both sessions, the difference, and the spread of the middle half of its times in both sessions.
- A segment is listed only when it has a typical time, so at least 3 laps through it, in both sessions. When the session before has too few laps for a lap-time spread, the page shows this session's spread alone. The numbers are the summary's own; they are observations from your laps, not causes.

### Where the laps vary

- The **Consistency** card on the day page has a new **Where the laps vary** map. It shows one session at a time, the latest unless you choose another above the map when the day has more than one. The best lap is drawn with each segment coloured by how much its times vary in that session (the spread of the middle half): green up to 0.10 s, light green to 0.25 s, yellow to 0.50 s, orange to 1.00 s and red above.
- Below the map, the segments are listed from the most varied, each with its spread. A segment with fewer than three laps in the session is grey and says so, and any part of the lap outside every segment is purple, marked *Not in a segment*. The bands are fixed seconds, so the same colour means the same spread on every day.

### Where a corner's time came from

- The corner details on the day page (tap a corner in **Where the time goes**) have a new **Where the time came from** section: the lap's time through the corner's **Entry** (from the corner's start to where it is tightest), **Middle** (through its tightest part) and **Exit** (on to the corner's end), against the best lap, for example *Middle 1.33 s · 1.59 s · −0.26 s*.
- The split comes from the track's shape, not from either lap's driving, so both laps are timed over the same metres and the three parts add up to the corner's time. A corner with more than one tight part (a double apex or a complex), one across the start/finish line, or one whose tightest part cannot be placed is not split, and the section says why. A lap with a gap in its recording anywhere in the corner is not timed through the parts, and the section says so, also when that lap is the best lap. The parts show where on the corner the time went, not why.

### Lap direction

- Before, one early pass across the start/finish line the wrong way (for example in the pit lane or while reversing) fixed the lap direction, so every real lap after it was rejected and the session could show no laps.
- Now, when passes the other way are rejected, the recording is scanned again in that direction, and the direction that finds more passes wins; on a tie, the direction with the faster passes wins. Recordings without such a pass are unchanged. Overlays still fixes the direction from the first pass.

### Reading recordings: duplicate channels

- Before, when a VBO file had two columns for the same channel (for example GPS speed and OBD speed, or a throttle and an accelerator pedal column), the first one by name was always used, even when it was almost empty, and an accelerator pedal column with a single value replaced a full throttle column.
- Now the column whose data covers more of the recording is used: the first by name is kept unless another covers more than twice its time, and a column whose values change is preferred over one that stays constant, unless it has only a few seconds of data. An accelerator pedal replaces the throttle only when it covers at least half the throttle's time. Recordings with only one candidate column per channel are unchanged. Overlays still takes the first column by name, so it may show a different column for the same file.

### Pedals recorded as 0 to 1 with no unit

- Before, a brake or throttle recorded with no unit and values between 0 and 1 was read as %, so it never reached the pedal thresholds: no throttle pickup, no throttle in the driving states, and braking only from deceleration (none without a usable longitudinal G).
- Now such a pedal is read as a 0 to 1 fraction only when the longitudinal G shows it: pressed in at least half of the hard brakings (for the brake) or hard accelerations (for the throttle), and released in at least half of the hard accelerations (for the brake) or hard brakings (for the throttle), with three or more of each. Corner braking, driving states, the throttle pickup and the coach then use it.
- Otherwise its scale is not guessed. A brake gives way to the deceleration when that is usable: braking is marked *inferred*, and the corner details say *Inferred from deceleration: the brake channel's scale (0–1 or %) is not known*. Any other such pedal shows *pedal scale unknown* in the driving states and *pedal scale (0–1 or %) not known* in the corner details, instead of no braking or throttle. The coach no longer uses such a pedal, where before it read the throttle as 0 to 1. Overlays does not judge the scale, so it can still show no braking or throttle for these recordings.

### User guide

- The **Corner Analyzer** page now says that braking is worked out from deceleration only when the session records a longitudinal G in g (or with no unit) that reaches at least 0.3 g of braking; without one, the brake channel is kept, unless its scale is not known. The coasting part of the **Driving** page now says that when the brake channel does not show the braking, braking is taken from longitudinal G and coasting is shown as inferred. The app is unchanged; this only corrects the guide.
