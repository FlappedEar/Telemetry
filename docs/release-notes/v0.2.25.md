## FlappedEar Telemetry 0.2.25 (pre-release)

A new **Grip and balance** card on the day page shows how hard the car was worked when cornering, braking and accelerating, per session and per corner. It installs over 0.2.24 and earlier versions and over Android builds from `main`, and keeps your saved days. From 0.2.6 on, **Settings › Check for updates** finds this release.

### Grip and balance

- The card sits below the G-G envelope and is closed at first; tap **Show** to open it. Every value is labelled **Inferred**: it comes from the recorded acceleration, speed and, when there is one, the car's yaw rate. No steering, tyre or wheel data is recorded, so it says how much force the laps reached, not how much grip was left. Only ranked laps count. *Typical* is the median of the laps and needs at least 3 laps; *peak* is the highest lap's value.
- **By session, at comparable speed**: pick a session to see its cornering, braking and accelerating in three speed bands (below 80, 80 to 120 and from 120 km/h; 50 and 75 for a recording in mph), so sessions are compared at similar speeds.
- **By corner**: each corner's typical cornering, braking and exit acceleration. Tap a corner for the peak, the lap it comes from and how many laps were used. A corner across the start/finish line is read from both of its stretches of the lap.
- **Balance**: the car's yaw rate against the yaw rate its cornering force and speed need. Above 1 the car rotated faster than its path curved, below 1 slower. It cannot tell understeer from oversteer, which needs the steering angle. It needs a yaw-rate channel of the car itself, so a RaceChrono recording shows it as *not known*.
- Units are kept as recorded, and an assumed unit is named. Without a longitudinal acceleration channel (as in a RaceChrono `.rcz`), braking and accelerating come from the change in speed and say *from speed*. A channel of zeros only is a placeholder and reads *not known*. Whatever cannot be worked out shows *not known* with the reason, never zero.

### Already in 0.2.24: laps near a crossing

- 0.2.24 also included this fix, which its notes left out. On a track that crosses itself, has parallel straights driven in opposite directions or a tight hairpin, a lap with a gap in its GPS fixes could pick up on the wrong part of the track afterwards, so the rest of the lap was placed a lap or more further on, or on the other leg of a hairpin. Such fixes are now left out instead, apart from a few at the very end of a lap or very close to the car. The real day it was tested on is unchanged.
