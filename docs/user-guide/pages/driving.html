G-G, driving states and coasting

Three cards under the Corner Analyzer on the comparison page. Each covers the whole lap, or only the stretch you zoomed the charts to (Selected stretch · 420 m).

G-G

  • Each dot is one moment of the lap: left/right is lateral G, up is accelerating, down is braking.
  • Rings are every 0.5 g. A circle marks each lap's peaks.
  • The table gives Peak lateral, Peak braking, Peak accelerating, Peak combined and the number of samples.
  • Under it, which channels were used and whether they were measured or calculated from GPS by the logger.
  • When neither lap has the G channels, the panel says why in place of the diagram.

These are observed accelerations, not a share of the grip available.

Driving states

Coloured strips show where each lap was braking, braking while cornering, cornering, accelerating or coasting. Tap a strip to move the cursor there. The table gives each state's time and share of the lap, and whether it was measured, calculated from GPS or inferred.

A pedal recorded with no unit whose values stay between 0 and 1 could be a 0–1 fraction or a pedal hardly pressed in %. It is read as a fraction only when the longitudinal G shows it: pressed in at least half of the hard brakings (or accelerations) and released in at least half of the hard accelerations (or brakings). Three or more of each are needed. Otherwise a brake gives way to the deceleration when that is usable (marked inferred), and any other such pedal shows pedal scale unknown instead of no braking or throttle; the coach does not use it either.

States overlap: cornering can come with braking, accelerating or coasting. Longer braking while cornering is not automatically better or safer.

Coasting

For each lap: total coasting time and distance, how it was measured, a strip along the track, and a chip for each episode such as 640 m · 1.2 s. Tap a chip to move the cursor there.

Coasting is measured from the brake and accelerator pedals when both were recorded. Without them it is inferred from longitudinal G, and the card says so. A single lap's coasting is also on its lap page.
