Corner Analyzer

On the comparison page, the Corner Analyzer goes through the track one segment at a time and puts lap A and lap B side by side.

Which segments

The analyzer uses the segments both laps share. Until the day is saved, these are the segments proposed from the best lap, and a note says so. If the two laps have no shared segments, the card says so and offers Use the theoretical best’s segments.

Picking a segment

Use the arrows (Previous segment, Next segment) or the menu, for example Corner 3 · corner · 142 m. Choosing a segment zooms all charts to it and puts the cursor in it.

Speed through the segment

The chart shows both laps' speed through the segment (shaded) and just around it. Markers along the bottom: ▲ braking point, ◆ throttle pickup, ● lowest speed, and a dashed line at the apex. Tap or drag sideways to move the cursor.

A missing value shows — with the reason, such as no brake channel or lap not fully covered here. Values worked out from deceleration instead of a pedal are marked inferred. The brake channel is used only when it shows the braking. When it has (almost) no data, or is not pressed in at least half of the hard brakings the longitudinal G shows (three or more are needed to judge), or its scale is not known (see below), the whole session's braking points and braking and coasting time are worked out from deceleration instead and marked inferred. This needs a longitudinal G recorded in g (or with no unit) that reaches at least 0.3 g of braking somewhere in the session; without one, the brake channel is kept, unless its scale is not known (see below). The brake chart and the Brake (measured) map layer in Compare still show the recorded brake. The corner details on the day page add the brake channel does not show the braking (or the brake channel's scale (0–1 or %) is not known), and the coach does not count it as a pedal. A brake recorded in another unit than % (a brake with no unit is judged as above) is not replaced unless it has (almost) no data: it shows channel unit not supported. A pedal with no unit whose scale nothing shows gives pedal scale (0–1 or %) not known (see G-G, driving states and coasting).

Zoom to segment zooms the charts again; Lap A here and Lap B here open a lap at the segment start.

Corner details from the day page

On the day page, tap a corner in the Where the time goes list for that lap's corner figures against the best lap:

  • Entry, minimum and exit speed; braking point before the corner; braking time; peak deceleration; throttle pickup.
  • Each figure says whether it came from the brake or throttle channel or was inferred.
  • Where the time came from: the lap's time through the corner's Entry (from the corner's start to where it is tightest), Middle (through its tightest part) and Exit (on to the corner's end), against the best lap, for example Middle 1.33 s · 1.59 s · −0.26 s. The split comes from the track's shape, not from either lap's driving, so both laps are timed over the same metres and the three add up to the corner's time. A corner with more than one tight part (a double apex or a complex), or one across the start/finish line, is not split, and the sheet says why. A lap with a gap in its recording anywhere in the corner is not timed through the parts, and when that is the best lap, the sheet says so. Braking before the corner and the straight after it are not part of the split; the Where the time goes list shows the straights.
  • Best of N laps: who had the highest minimum and exit speed, the latest braking point and the earliest pickup.
  • Open in the Corner Analyzer compares the lap with the best lap through this corner.

Later braking or an earlier pickup is not automatically faster. The parts show where on the corner the time went, not why.
