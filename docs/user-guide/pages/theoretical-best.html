Theoretical best and time losses

The day page splits the track into segments (corners and straights) and times every ranked lap through each one. That shows what is left and where each lap loses it.

Theoretical best

  • Best lap: the fastest lap of the group.
  • Theoretical best: the fastest time of each segment, across all laps, added up.
  • Available: the difference.

It combines parts of different laps, so it does not show that the whole lap can be driven that fast. While it is being worked out, the card says Timing every lap on one track axis….

Under them, Three ways to add it up:

  • Fastest segments: the theoretical best above.
  • Segments that join: an estimate of the fastest segments that could follow one another. Wherever a segment from one lap meets a segment from another, the speeds there must differ by at most 2 km/h, because a car cannot change speed at a line. Segments of the same lap always follow one another, and when the last segment runs across the start/finish line into the first, that join counts too. The line says how many laps it is made of. It is never quicker than the fastest segments; when the best lap has a time through every segment, it is never slower than the best lap either. Matching speeds does not prove the two parts could be driven together (two laps can take different lines at the same speed), and at 10 recordings a second 2 km/h is close to what the timing of a line can tell apart under braking, so read it as an estimate. When no laps join at every line, or no lap records speed, it says so.
  • Best typical: each segment's quickest typical (median) time in one session, added up: what you did through each segment as a rule, not once. It needs at least three laps through each segment in one session (not necessarily the same session for every segment); otherwise it says so.

On the Jastrząb day pictured, the fastest segments add up to 1:47.905, the segments that join to 1:48.581 from 7 laps, and the best typical to 1:50.711, against a best lap of 1:49.898.

Edit segments opens the segment editor.

When there is no theoretical best, the card says why. If the calculation failed, or there was nothing to calculate from, Calculate again runs it once more; the segment editor offers it too. When the calculation failed, the cards built on it (Where to look next, Time losses, Consistency and Progression) say only Not available: the theoretical best could not be calculated.

Where the time goes

  1. Choose a lap in the menu (the best lap is chosen first).
  2. The map colours each segment by how much time that lap loses there to the fastest time, from dim grey to bright red.
  3. The list below gives the segments, largest loss first: the loss, the fastest time and who set it, and for a corner the lap's minimum speed and braking point against the best lap, and what kind of corner it is, for example Heavy braking · Slow corner · Single apex (see corner type).
  • Tap a corner for its corner details.
  • Tap the compare button on a row to compare this lap with the best lap through that segment in the Corner Analyzer.

Sector times

Every lap by segment, with each segment's full name above its column. The fastest time of each segment is highlighted, the best lap of the day is marked · best after its name, and the last row, Fastest, adds up to the theoretical best. Tap a lap to show its losses on the map above. On a phone the table scrolls sideways while the lap column stays in place. A screen reader reads each row as one line, naming every column with its time.

Braking technique

Under the sector table, Braking technique sums up the day's braking in one line: the typical initial hit, release, trail braking (inferred) and brake-to-throttle time over the corners braked into, each the median of the corners' typical values, and whether they come from the G channel or from speed (with no unit recorded: read as … when the channel declares none). Corners measured another way (G or speed, or another unit) are never mixed in, and the line says how many were left out. A corner whose figure rests on fewer than half of its braking laps (say brake to throttle on 5 of 16, the others braking again before the throttle) is left out of that figure, and the line says how many. A value from speed that is only a lower bound reads at least, and a brake-to-throttle time from a throttle slower than about 10 Hz reads about, to 0.1 s. Tap a corner in Where the time goes for its braking lap by lap.

Lap to lap in each corner

Under the braking technique, each corner lists how much it changes from lap to lap over the group's laps. Tap a corner to see all its lines:

  • Braking point and Throttle pickup: the spread in metres, each marked measured (from the recorded brake or throttle) or inferred (from speed). The two are never mixed.
  • Apex speed, Minimum speed, Exit speed: the typical speed and its spread.
  • Line spread: how far apart the laps' lines are where the corner starts (entry), at its apex (or its middle, for a corner with several) and where it ends (exit), next to the recording's GPS accuracy, for example Line spread: entry 2.3 m, apex 1.8 m, exit 1.1 m · GPS accuracy about 0.15 m. A part with fewer than three laps is left out. When the recording states its GPS accuracy, the parts whose spread is no larger than it are named as not distinguishable from GPS error. The spread is the middle half of the laps' sideways distances from the reference lap's line; it shows where your line changes from lap to lap, not which line is faster.

Typical is the median and spread the middle half of the laps (interquartile range). Each figure needs at least 3 laps; with fewer the line says too few laps, and a figure no lap measured is left out. A corner with none says Not measured on these laps. These are observations of what changed, not their causes.

Best phases

Under the theoretical best, Best phases builds a target from the day's best corner parts: the best entry of one lap, the best middle of another, the best exit of a third. It is closed at first; its one line gives the total. Tap Show to open it.

  • Each corner is split where it is tightest into entry, middle and exit, the same split as in the corner details, so every lap is timed over the same metres. Each part comes from the ranked lap that was fastest through it.
  • A straight comes whole from the lap fastest through it, and so does a corner that is not split. That corner says why, for example Not split: more than one tight part for a run of corners you merged or one saved by an earlier version, such as Corners 2–3, or Not split: crosses the start/finish line for a corner on the start line.
  • Laps that are not ranked are left out.

At the top, Best phases is the parts added up; next to it is the chosen lap's time over the same segments and what it loses. Against the theoretical bests puts it beside the three totals of the Theoretical best card:

  • Fastest segments is the same idea a whole segment at a time. Best phases cuts each corner into three, so it is not slower: a corner's three parts can come from three laps, where the theoretical best takes the whole corner from one. The card says so only when its total shows it. A corner is cut only when every lap timed through it is timed through its parts; otherwise it is taken whole, and says why.
  • Best phases that join applies the rule of segments that join to the parts: wherever a part from one lap meets a part from another, at the lines inside a corner as well as between segments, the speeds must differ by at most 2 km/h. It is not slower than Segments that join, the same rule a whole segment at a time; the card says so only when its total shows it. A part's speed without a unit is read as km/h, and the card says so.
  • Best typical is a different idea: what you did through each segment as a rule, not once. It is not split into parts.

Where laps meet counts the lines where two laps meet and lists those where the speeds differ by more than 2 km/h, for example Straight 3 → Corner 4 · Entry: 2.8 km/h apart. There the best phases is not a lap the car drove. Lines where a lap's speed is not known are counted apart. As for segments that join, matching speeds are needed for two laps to join, but do not prove that they could: two laps can take different lines at the same speed.

Part by part lists the segments in lap order for the lap chosen in the menu (the best lap first). Each segment shows what that lap loses there; a split corner shows the same for its entry, middle and exit. Each line gives the best time and the lap that set it, and the chosen lap's own time, or this lap set it. A part whose lap does not join the part before says so in red, with the speed difference. A loss is never shown as zero when the lap has no time there: it reads —.

Best phases is made of parts of different laps, not one recording, so it is compared part by part here. It is not offered as a reference lap: a Δ trace against it would join traces the car never drove.

On the Jastrząb day pictured, the best phases add up to 1:46.060 from 9 laps and the best phases that join to 1:46.802 from 8 laps, against fastest segments of 1:47.905, segments that join of 1:48.581, a best typical of 1:50.892 and a best lap of 1:49.898. At 9 of the 19 lines where two laps meet, the speeds differ by more than 2 km/h.

Time losses

The largest single losses to the best lap, one segment at a time, for Each session's best lap or for Every lap. The first eight are shown; Show all lists the rest. On a day with one session, its best lap is the best lap of the day, so Each session's best compares nothing and the card says so: choose Every lap. When no other lap could be timed through the segments, the card says that instead.

Tap a loss for both laps' times through the segment, where the gap grew, the segment on the map, and buttons to open the lap or Compare with the best lap there.

Where to look next

Up to three places picked from the measured losses, sector gaps and corner spreads. Each says what kind it is (Best lap against the fastest sector, Repeated loss, Braking-point spread or Lowest-speed spread), what was Observed, a Hypothesis to check, and which two laps to compare.

Tap an area for its detail page, with the segment on the map and buttons to open either lap or Compare laps A and B. A hypothesis is something to check in the laps, not a cause or an instruction.

For a day kept in your library, each area also gets an Earlier visits here line under its hypothesis, from your earlier days at this track in this car (a later day, today and other cars do not count). An undated day counts every dated day as earlier. When the area's segment is one of the track's corners in your library, it names the corner as your library does and says on how many of the visits that measured it the corner cost time, with the date of the last one, for example Earlier visits here (Turn 4): it cost time on 2 of 3 visits, last on Aug 29, 2026. A corner costs time on a visit when it is among the three corners that cost the most time that day, by 0.1 s or more, the rule of Corners that keep costing time. If the last two visits that measured it did not lose time there, it says but not on the last 2 visits that measured it; a corner measured but never among those three says so. Otherwise the line says why there is nothing to show: none in this car in your library on your first visit here in this car, this corner was not measured before when earlier days did not measure it, and this place is not one of the track's corners in your library yet when the segment is not one of them (a straight, for example). Like the rest of the card, it is an observation, not a cause. A day saved outside the library, one whose track was not recognised, or one whose corners could not be placed on the ground (no usable GPS) shows no line, as Last time here.
