Consistency, progression and the day report

How the day went as a whole: how steady the laps were, how each session compared with the one before, how hard each session worked the car in each direction, the car's temperatures and your heart rate, and one page that sums it up.

Consistency

For the whole day and for each session: the typical lap (median) and the spread (the width of the middle half of the laps), so one slow or quick lap does not dominate. When only one session has laps to measure, there is one row, named after that session. Then the same for each segment. At least three laps are needed; otherwise it says so.

Where the laps vary shows one session at a time, the latest first; choose another above the map. The best lap is drawn on the map with each segment coloured by its spread in that session: green up to 0.10 s, then light green to 0.25 s, yellow to 0.50 s, orange to 1.00 s and red above. Below the map the segments are listed from the most varied, each with its spread. A segment with fewer than three laps in the session is grey and says so. Any part of the lap outside every segment is drawn in purple, marked Not in a segment. The bands are fixed seconds, so the same colour means the same spread on every day.

Progression

  • By session: each session in order with its start time, best lap, laps ranked, typical lap, the best lap against the session before (for example Best −0.412 s against Session 1), and a bar of its lap times (range, middle half, median). Tap a session to open its best lap.
  • By segment: a table of each segment's typical time per session, the quickest highlighted. Tap a cell to see its laps.
  • By lap: every timed lap of each session in order, on a chart and in a table.

By lap

How lap times move through each session and from one session to the next. The chart has one line per session, lap time by lap number, with slower laps higher. Each session has its own marker and its name at the end of its line; earlier sessions are also fainter. The table has a row per session and a column per lap number, so a column is the same point of every session. Each cell shows the lap time and when the lap began after the start of the session's first timed lap, such as +2:31. The clock starts at the first timed lap, not the recording: a recording can start in the paddock long before the out lap. Out and in laps are not timed laps and are not shown. A lap left out of the ranking (off the route, excluded by you, incomplete GPS) is shown as not ranked, breaks its session's line and is never measured; below the table it is listed with the reason, as in the lap list, for example LAP 4 · Not ranked: Lap leaves the route the other laps took (off track, a detour or the pit lane). Tap a lap to open it.

Below the table, for each session:

  • Air (modelled): the air temperature from the weather model, when the session's weather is kept.
  • First lap in the session's middle half or quicker, and after how many laps: the first ranked lap no slower than the slow edge of the middle half of the session's ranked lap times (the upper quartile), so it takes at least three ranked laps. Laps before it are grey in the table. If some of those laps are not ranked, it says so: whether they were slower is not known. This is where the session settled into its usual times, not its full pace: when a later lap was quicker than it by more than the session's spread (the width of the middle half), it names that lap, for example Its quickest lap came later: LAP 4, 13.644 s quicker than LAP 2. It says nothing about the laps in between.
  • Against the session before at the same laps: the typical (median) difference between its laps and the previous session's laps with the same number, where both are ranked, for example Against Session 4 at the same laps: −10.214 s typical difference over 4 laps. A negative difference means it was quicker. It needs at least three such laps, otherwise it says how many there are.

These are observations, not causes. The tyres warming up, the track rubbering in or drying, the weather and you learning the track all change together through a session and through the day, and this cannot tell them apart. The track temperature is not recorded, and the air temperature comes from a weather model for the area, not from the track. The Car card says how a temperature the car recorded moved with lap time, and flags it when the temperature just follows the order of the day.

Each session's weather, conditions, setup changes, setup and notes are shown when it has them, the setup on one line such as Setup: Cold 2.1 / 2.1 / 2 / 2 bar · Tyres Pirelli SC2 · Fuel 8.5 l in the unit it was entered in. Enter them in Session details; conditions, setup changes and notes can also come from FlappedEar Overlays.

G-G envelope

How hard each session worked the car in each direction (combined G). The diagram has accelerating at the top and braking at the bottom; left is turning left and right is turning right, as on the Compare G-G. For each of eight directions (accelerating, braking, turning left or right, and braking or accelerating while turning), the app takes the combined G of the samples pointing that way and keeps its 95th percentile, so a single spike does not set it. Only the ranked laps of the group shown count: out laps, in laps and laps you excluded are left out, as on the other cards. Samples below 0.1 g have no clear direction and are left out too, and so are readings beyond 4 g, which are not plausible for a road car; the card says how many a session lost that way.

  • Each session is a line in its own colour, earlier sessions in blue and green, later ones in yellow; the latest session is the thickest. The Day's best in each direction is the dashed purple line. A direction without a value breaks the line instead of being bridged.
  • The table lists each direction with the day's best and the session it came from, next to the latest session. A latest-session value in red and bold is unused envelope: at least 0.1 g below the day's best in that direction. The line under the table names each one with both values and the session of the best, or says that the latest session came within 0.1 g of the day's best in every direction it has a value for.
  • A direction with fewer than 25 samples shows —. A session with too little data, without G channels or without its recording says so by name instead of showing a line; when no session has an envelope, the card says that.

The card names the G channels and how they were obtained: measured, or calculated from GPS by the logger for RaceChrono's calculated channels (longacc-calc, latacc-calc), and says when the recording does not declare their units. The values are observed accelerations, not a share of the grip available: a lower envelope can come from traffic, a wet track or a different line as well as from the driver.

Grip and balance

Below the G-G envelope, the Grip and balance card shows how hard the car was worked on the ranked laps of the compared group. It is closed at first, with one line; tap Show to open it and Hide to close it again. Every value on it is labelled Inferred: it comes from the recorded acceleration, speed and, when there is one, the car's yaw rate. No steering angle, tyre or wheel data is recorded, so the card says how much force the laps reached, not how much grip was left. Out laps, in laps and laps that are not ranked are left out. Typical is the median of the laps and needs at least 3 laps measured the same way; peak is the highest lap's value.

  • By session, at comparable speed: pick a session; a table shows its cornering (sideways acceleration), braking and accelerating in three speed bands (below 80, 80 to 120, and from 120 km/h; 50 and 75 for a recording in mph), so sessions are compared at similar speeds; for a recording whose speed declares no unit the bands are in km/h and say km/h assumed. Each cell is the typical of the laps' highest values in that band, with the highest lap's value under it. A band needs at least 20 samples on a lap to count.
  • By corner: each of the day's corners with its typical cornering, braking and exit acceleration. Tap a corner for the peak, the lap it comes from and how many laps were used. Braking is the highest deceleration from the braking point to the slowest point of the corner; exit acceleration is the mean from the slowest point to the end of the corner (below zero the car was still slowing). A lap needs at least 5 samples in that part of the corner. A corner that crosses the start/finish line is read from both of its stretches of the lap: from its start to the end of the lap and from the start of the lap to its end. When a corner has too few laps for a typical value, its line shows the peak; opened, it also says how many ranked laps had no value there.
  • Balance: the car's yaw rate divided by the yaw rate its cornering force and speed need, read where the car corners at 0.3 g or more and 10 m/s or faster (at least 10 such samples on a lap). Above 1 the car rotated faster than its path curved (its slip angle growing); below 1, slower. In steady cornering the ratio is about 1 whether the car understeers or oversteers: telling those apart needs the steering angle, which is not recorded. Samples where the yaw rate turns against the cornering are left out. When the lateral acceleration's or the speed's unit is assumed, the balance says units assumed. It needs a yaw-rate channel of the car itself. A phone or logger gyro measures the device's own axes, not the car's, so a RaceChrono recording shows balance as not known.

Units are kept as the recording declares them. When an acceleration channel declares no unit, g is assumed and the card says so. Without a longitudinal acceleration channel (a RaceChrono RCZ import has none), braking and accelerating come from the change in speed and the card says that too (from speed, and km/h assumed when the speed declares no unit); without a lateral acceleration channel, cornering is not known. Laps measured with different channels or units are never mixed: the card says how many were left out. An acceleration channel that holds only zeros is a placeholder (RaceChrono writes them), not a measurement, and is shown as not known. Whatever cannot be worked out is shown as not known with the reason, never as zero.

Lap styles

Below Grip and balance, the Lap styles card groups the ranked laps of the compared group by how they were driven, and shows the best lap of each group. It is closed at first, with one line; tap Show to open it and Hide to close it again. It is labelled Inferred: the groups come from figures the Corner Analyzer already measures, not from a new measurement. Out laps, in laps, laps you excluded and laps with issues are not grouped; the card says how many of the group's timed laps were.

Each lap is compared with the day's typical lap at every corner, where typical is the median of the day's laps and needs at least 3 laps measured the same way. Four things are compared:

  • Braking point: where braking started, in metres before the corner. It is read from the longitudinal acceleration (or from the speed's slope when there is none), never from the brake pedal, which a recording made through the car's OBD port updates only about twice a second. The card says when the acceleration or speed used declares no unit and is read as g, km/h or mph (naming the unit it read), and when no braking point could be read at all.
  • Throttle pickup: where the throttle came back after the corner's entry, in metres, as the Corner Analyzer measures it. A throttle recorded through OBD updates about twice a second, so it shows the pickup to within roughly 10 m.
  • Minimum speed and exit speed, in the unit the recording declares, never converted. Speeds in different units are not compared. When a speed declares no unit, the card says which unit the settings assumed for it, or, when none is set, shows the speeds without one.

A lap's group comes from plain counting rules. "Most corners" means more than half of the corners where the lap has a figure, and at least 3 of them; earlier or later means more than 5 m for braking and more than 10 m for the throttle.

  • Conservative: braking earlier and the throttle later than typical, each in most corners.
  • Late braking: braking later than typical in most corners.
  • Early throttle: the throttle earlier than typical in most corners.
  • Mixed: late braking together with early throttle; late braking with the throttle mostly later, or early throttle with the braking mostly earlier; or earlier braking or later throttle without the other half of conservative.
  • Typical: none of those.
  • Outlier: braking or throttle at least three times those distances from typical in more than half of its corners, or measured in fewer corners than it takes to tell: 3, or half the corners compared on the day when that is more. The lap says which, and how many corners it takes.

Speeds do not decide a group, because they follow a lap's pace: a slower lap is not a different style. They are shown for comparison.

The typical at a corner is the median of the grouped laps measured there the same way, at least 3 of them, and it includes the lap being compared, so a lap's difference from it is somewhat damped. Laps from a session that recorded a channel or unit another way are not pooled with the others, so a typical can rest on fewer laps than were grouped. Tap a group to see its laps. Each group shows how many laps it has, its best lap and how far that is from the day's best lap, a typical lap time (from 3 laps), and how many of its laps are among the quicker half of the day's laps (the quickest half of the grouped laps, rounded up, with any lap tied with the last of them). Under that, its best lap is set against the day's typical: at how many corners it braked earlier or later, picked up the throttle earlier or later, and was faster or slower, each with the median difference. Tap a lap in the list to open it.

The groups are observations, not causes. Few laps rest on each group (a session has three to five), and a day's laps also quicken as the driver learns the track, so the groups mix driving style with progress. A quick lap in a group does not show that the style made it quick; read the groups as a pointer to what to look at. Whatever cannot be worked out, such as a day with fewer than 3 ranked laps or with braking and throttle measured at fewer than 3 corners, is said so, never shown as a group.

Car and Driver

The Car card summarises each recorded temperature (oil, coolant, intake air, gearbox, exhaust, ambient) per session: mean, range and how much was covered, with implausible readings left out. With at least eight laps it also says how the temperature moved with lap time. Tap a channel for every section and its cooling, such as Cooling: −12 °C in 1:40 (in lap).

The Driver card shows each session's heart rate, mean and range, and each lap's mean; tap a lap to open it. Both cards say so when nothing was recorded. If the summaries could not be calculated, both cards say why, with Calculate again.

Day report

Tap the Report tab under the day's name. One page with:

  • Best lap and what is left: best lap, theoretical best and the time available, with Open best lap.
  • Where to look next and the Largest time losses (top five).
  • Sessions, Consistency, Car and Heart rate.

The report shows what the day page has worked out so far. A part that is missing says why: Calculating…, Not calculated yet. or Unavailable. Tapping an entry opens its lap.

Sharing the report

To send the day to an instructor, tap Share as image under the group's name. The whole report becomes one picture (PNG) headed by the day's name, followed by the coach's Next session notes, without buttons or maps, in the look and language the app uses. On a phone or tablet the share sheet opens, so you can send it by message or mail or save it to your photos. On a computer you choose where to save it, and the app says Report saved as an image. The button appears once the report has a group of laps, and waits while a part of the report or the coach is still being calculated, so the picture never says Calculating…. If the picture cannot be made, the app says why, for example when the report is too long for one image.
