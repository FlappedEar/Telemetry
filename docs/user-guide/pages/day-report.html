Consistency, progression and the day report

How the day went as a whole: how steady the laps were, how each session compared with the one before, how hard each session worked the car in each direction, the car's temperatures and your heart rate, and one page that sums it up.

Consistency

For the whole day and for each session: the typical lap (median) and the spread (the width of the middle half of the laps), so one slow or quick lap does not dominate. When only one session has laps to measure, there is one row, named after that session. Then the same for each segment. At least three laps are needed; otherwise it says so.

Where the laps vary shows one session at a time, the latest first; choose another above the map. The best lap is drawn on the map with each segment coloured by its spread in that session: green up to 0.10 s, then light green to 0.25 s, yellow to 0.50 s, orange to 1.00 s and red above. Below the map the segments are listed from the most varied, each with its spread. A segment with fewer than three laps in the session is grey and says so. Any part of the lap outside every segment is drawn in purple, marked Not in a segment. The bands are fixed seconds, so the same colour means the same spread on every day.

Progression

  • By session: each session in order with its start time, best lap, laps ranked, typical lap, the best lap against the session before (for example Best −0.412 s against Session 1), and a bar of its lap times (range, middle half, median). Tap a session to open its best lap.
  • By segment: a table of each segment's typical time per session, the quickest highlighted. Tap a cell to see its laps.
  • By lap: every timed lap of each session in order, on a chart and in a table.

By lap

How lap times move through each session and from one session to the next. The chart has one line per session, lap time by lap number, with slower laps higher and earlier sessions fainter. The table has a row per session and a column per lap number, so a column is the same point of every session. Each cell shows the lap time and when the lap began after the start of the session's first timed lap, such as +2:31. The clock starts at the first timed lap, not the recording: a recording can start in the paddock long before the out lap. Out and in laps are not timed laps and are not shown. A lap left out of the ranking (off the route, excluded by you, incomplete GPS) is shown as not counted, breaks its session's line and is never measured. Tap a lap to open it.

Below the table, for each session:

  • Air: the modelled air temperature, when the session's weather is kept.
  • At its pace from LAP n, after how many laps. A lap is at the session's pace when it is no slower than the slow edge of the middle half of the session's ranked laps (the upper quartile), so it takes at least three ranked laps. Laps before it are grey in the table. If some of those laps are not counted, it says so: whether they were slower is not known.
  • Against the session before at the same laps: the typical (median) difference between its laps and the previous session's laps with the same number, where both are ranked, for example Against Session 4 at the same laps: −10.214 s typical difference over 4 laps. A negative difference means it was quicker. It needs at least three such laps, otherwise it says how many there are.

These are observations, not causes. The tyres warming up, the track rubbering in or drying, the weather and you learning the track all change together through a session and through the day, and this cannot tell them apart. The track temperature is not recorded, and the air temperature comes from a weather model for the area, not from the track. The Car card says how a temperature the car recorded moved with lap time, and flags it when the temperature just follows the order of the day.

Each session's weather, conditions, setup changes, setup and notes are shown when it has them, the setup on one line such as Setup: Cold 2.1 / 2.1 / 2 / 2 bar · Tyres Pirelli SC2 · Fuel 8.5 l in the unit it was entered in. Enter them in Session details; conditions, setup changes and notes can also come from FlappedEar Overlays.

G-G envelope

How hard each session worked the car in each direction (combined G). The diagram has accelerating at the top and braking at the bottom; left is turning left and right is turning right, as on the Compare G-G. For each of eight directions (accelerating, braking, turning left or right, and braking or accelerating while turning), the app takes the combined G of the samples pointing that way and keeps its 95th percentile, so a single spike does not set it. Only the ranked laps of the group shown count: out laps, in laps and laps you excluded are left out, as on the other cards. Samples below 0.1 g have no clear direction and are left out too, and so are readings beyond 4 g, which are not plausible for a road car; the card says how many a session lost that way.

  • Each session is a line in its own colour, earlier sessions in blue and green, later ones in yellow; the latest session is the thickest. The Day's best in each direction is the dashed purple line. A direction without a value breaks the line instead of being bridged.
  • The table lists each direction with the day's best and the session it came from, next to the latest session. A latest-session value in red and bold is unused envelope: at least 0.1 g below the day's best in that direction. The line under the table names each one with both values and the session of the best, or says that the latest session came within 0.1 g of the day's best in every direction it has a value for.
  • A direction with fewer than 25 samples shows —. A session with too little data, without G channels or without its recording says so by name instead of showing a line; when no session has an envelope, the card says that.

The card names the G channels and how they were obtained: measured, or calculated from GPS by the logger for RaceChrono's calculated channels (longacc-calc, latacc-calc), and says when the recording does not declare their units. The values are observed accelerations, not a share of the grip available: a lower envelope can come from traffic, a wet track or a different line as well as from the driver.

Car and Driver

The Car card summarises each recorded temperature (oil, coolant, intake air, gearbox, exhaust, ambient) per session: mean, range and how much was covered, with implausible readings left out. With at least eight laps it also says how the temperature moved with lap time. Tap a channel for every section and its cooling, such as Cooling: −12 °C in 1:40 (in lap).

The Driver card shows each session's heart rate, mean and range, and each lap's mean; tap a lap to open it. Both cards say so when nothing was recorded. If the summaries could not be calculated, both cards say why, with Calculate again.

Day report

Tap the Report tab under the day's name. One page with:

  • Best lap and what is left: best lap, theoretical best and the time available, with Open best lap.
  • Where to look next and the Largest time losses (top five).
  • Sessions, Consistency, Car and Heart rate.

The report shows what the day page has worked out so far. A part that is missing says why: Calculating…, Not calculated yet. or Unavailable. Tapping an entry opens its lap.

Sharing the report

To send the day to an instructor, tap Share as image under the group's name. The whole report becomes one picture (PNG) headed by the day's name, followed by the coach's Next session notes, without buttons or maps, in the look and language the app uses. On a phone or tablet the share sheet opens, so you can send it by message or mail or save it to your photos. On a computer you choose where to save it, and the app says Report saved as an image. The button appears once the report has a group of laps, and waits while a part of the report or the coach is still being calculated, so the picture never says Calculating…. If the picture cannot be made, the app says why, for example when the report is too long for one image.
