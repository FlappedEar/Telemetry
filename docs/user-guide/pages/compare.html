Compare laps

Two laps at the same place on the track. Lap A is amber, lap B is blue, and Δ is A − B: positive when A is behind.

Opening a comparison

  • The Compare tab under the day's name: Compare two laps, or tap a suggestion under Against the best of the day, which puts each session's best lap (A, amber) against the best lap of the day (B, blue), with the gap on the right.
  • Compare two laps above the lap list: choose lap A, then lap B. Suggested: the fastest picks the group's best lap.
  • Compare with… on a lap: that lap is A; choose B.
  • From the analysis (a time loss, a corner, a focus area): the comparison opens zoomed to that segment, with the Corner Analyzer first.
  • Last comparison above the lap list: the two laps compared last, zoomed to the same stretch and with the same charts. The comparison is saved with the day, so it is there when the day opens again, also when it was set up in FlappedEar Overlays. It is not offered when one of the laps has changed or is no longer ranked. Because the comparison is saved with the day, opening a different pair, zooming or changing the charts leaves the day with unsaved changes.

Only ranked laps of the same group can be compared.

Choosing A and B

  • Tap the A or B badge to pick another lap. Under each lap's time is its session's weather, such as 18 °C, overcast, no rain, wind SW 12 km/h, when the app has it.
  • Swap A and B.
  • B: best of Session N (best of A's session) and B: best of the day. If A is that lap already, the next fastest is used.
  • Lap Δ is the difference in lap time.

The comparison map

Both lines are drawn on the same street, satellite or Apple Maps background as the other maps, with a dot for each lap at the cursor. Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag to move (on a phone or tablet, use two fingers), and use the layers button to change the background. The menu above the map colours a line by:

  • Line: A / B (default): amber and blue.
  • Speed, Δ time (A−B), Lateral G, Longitudinal G, Throttle, Brake (measured), and each recorded temperature.

When a colouring is chosen, A / B next to the menu picks which lap's line is coloured. If a channel was not recorded, the menu says not recorded; nothing is invented.

Channels by track position

The charts put both laps at the same place on the track, in metres from the start/finish line.

  • The first chart is Δ time (A − B): rising means A is losing time there. Each lap is timed from its own start, so where both laps are recorded up to the line, Δ time is 0 at the start/finish line and equals the Lap Δ at the finish.
  • Then speed, throttle and brake when both laps recorded them. Add, remove or swap channels from a chart's name as on the lap page (up to four). A channel that came from an RCZ in any session of the day says from RCZ.
  • Tap a chart or drag sideways across it to move the cursor; both dots move on the map. With a mouse or trackpad, the cursor follows the pointer.
  • A heading chart is one continuous line through north; the value at the cursor is the recorded 0 to 360, and a screen reader says the heading from where it starts to where it ends, or 0 to 360 when the lap turns all the way round (see the lap page).
  • With a screen reader (TalkBack or VoiceOver), each chart says each lap's lowest and highest value in the stretch shown.
  • Zoom in around the cursor to look at one corner; the map highlights the stretch shown.
  • Open lap A here / Open lap B here open that lap at the same place.

Under the charts

  • Corner Analyzer: corner by corner, A against B.
  • G-G, Driving states and Coasting for the whole lap, or for the stretch you zoomed to.

Everything on this page is an observed difference between two laps, not an instruction.
