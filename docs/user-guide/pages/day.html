Day results

The day page leads with your best lap and its map, then explains where the time is. Tabs under the day's name switch between Overview, Laps, Compare and Report; on a wide screen the overview is on the left and the laps on the right, so the tabs are Overview, Compare and Report. Report is the whole day on one page; see Day report. The app's own places (Home, Library, Day, Coach, Profile) stay in the bar at the bottom or the rail at the side; see Home. Coach shows this day's Next session card and, below it, your skill levels across days from your profile.

The toolbar

  • The title is Day results for a day never saved, otherwise the day's name. A • after the name means there are unsaved changes.
  • Save (disk icon): the day saves itself in your library, and this saves at once. Under More (⋮) on a Mac or PC, Export for Overlays… (Save as… for a day opened from a file). See Saving.
  • Under More (⋮), Add and review recordings…: add recordings after choosing what happens to each. See Adding with a review.
  • Add recordings (list icon with a plus): add the next session to this day. See Adding a session.
  • Settings (gear): on a narrow screen, such as a phone, it is under More (⋮), so the day's name has room.

Adding a session

At the track you do not need to import the whole day again after each session.

  1. On the day page, tap Add recordings and choose the new VBO/RCZ file. On a phone you can also share it from RaceChrono; see Sharing from RaceChrono.
  2. Only the new file is read. It becomes the next session (Session 4 after three), and every card, ranking and the best day update.
  3. A message says Session 4 added to the day. and the page moves to Coach and its Next session card, which coaches the session recorded last: the one just added, unless it was recorded before the others. A recording already in the day is not added again. The RCZ of a session added as VBO joins that session as its alternative source instead (RCZ added to Session 2., see VBO and RCZ of one session); a VBO for a session added as RCZ is not added. The message says so.
  4. While the bar shows a session being added, the day stays open: Back asks you to wait until the session is in.

Excluded laps, circuit names and your chosen group are kept. A day that was saved is saved again at once with the new session; a day never saved is kept as unsaved work (see Restoring unsaved work).

Adding with a review

Add recordings needs no approval. To choose yourself, open More (⋮) › Add and review recordings… and pick the files. The bar under the toolbar shows while they are read, then Review the import lists them as described in Reviewing the files, with these differences:

  • Same run as also offers the day's sessions (Same run as Session 2) that have no other recording in any form yet (combined, kept beside it, or saved but not found): the file is kept with that session as its alternative recording. A session's other recording is never replaced from here.
  • A file the day already has says Already in this day; skipped.
  • At the top, Add to this day (the default) or Start a new day. A new day closes this one and imports the files as a day of their own, which then opens. It is only available when this day has no unsaved changes: Save this day before starting a new one. If another import is still running, this day stays open and says Finish the current import first. Nothing was imported. Changing where the files go starts the choices again from the automatic ones.
  • Add to the day (or Start the new day) confirms. If a session was added to the day meanwhile, for example one shared from RaceChrono, or the files changed, nothing is added and the message asks you to review them again.

Best lap of the day

The page opens with two bars. The amber Best lap of the day bar names that lap and its time; tap it to open the lap. The blue Theoretical best bar adds up the fastest time of every segment, once it has been calculated (see Theoretical best). Below them, the best lap's trace is coloured by speed (purple slow, yellow fast) with the speed range underneath; tap it to open the lap too.

If there is no best lap, the card says why, for example that no session has enough complete GPS laps to identify its circuit. In that case Set the circuit… opens the circuit of the first such session (see Circuits).

Next session

Between sessions, the Next session card, under Coach in the bar or rail, suggests what to try in the next run. It looks at the session recorded last, or the one added last when that recording has no time (the line under the heading names it, for example Coaching Session 6 against the day's faster laps), and compares its laps with the faster laps of the whole day, corner by corner. It compares each lap with up to two faster laps, preferring laps at most 1.5 s faster than it, so a suggestion is usually something you have already done close to your pace; when fewer than two are that close, it uses the faster laps nearest in lap time. Without a best lap there is nothing to compare with, and Coach says why instead.

  • Above the suggestions, Main focus from Session 5 checks how this session did on the main focus the coach gave for the session before, for example Corners 5–6 · Reduce coasting. Longest coast: 2.1 s then, 1.6 s in this session. Better. The values are the median across each session's laps at that corner (for the braking marker, the range from the earliest to the latest braking point; for a throttle picked up too early, the share of the session's laps picking it up early, where fewer is better), leaving out laps much slower than their session's typical lap. It says Better or Worse only for a clear change: 8 m for a lift or throttle-return point, 0.4 s of coasting, about 5 km/h (1.4 m/s) of minimum speed, 10 m of braking range, 25 points of the share of laps picking up the throttle early; otherwise About the same. The focus is the one the coach gave then: it is worked out again from the sessions up to the session before. All of each session's laps are measured, not only the laps that showed the pattern. The corners can be drawn differently now than then (they follow the day's best lap until you save the day); the values are then measured at today's corner overlapping the focus most, and the card says which, for example Measured at Corners 5–8, as today's corners divide the track. When no corner of today's overlaps it by at least half, it says so instead. The braking range grows with the number of laps, so it compares best between sessions of a similar length. With fewer than two laps to measure in either session (three for the braking range), it says Not measured in this session. It is an observation, not proof that the advice worked.
  • The first item is your Main focus: the one thing to work on in the next run. Any others are marked Once that feels settled: try them only when the main focus comes naturally. Each item names the corner and what to do, for example Corner 4 · Keep more speed through the slow point.
  • Measured says what the laps show, for example Minimum speed: 45.8 km/h on this session's laps, 52.7 km/h on your faster laps.
  • Try (or Keep, for an improvement over your last three laps) says what to work on. It never gives an exact point, and apart from the braking-marker item the braking point stays as it is.
  • Why? opens the measured values (session against faster laps), the laps they come from (This session's laps; Earlier laps showing it today lists laps of earlier sessions with the same pattern), the corner highlighted on the best lap's map (for a change at a lift, a braking start, a throttle return or a first throttle pickup, with this session's typical point in amber and the faster laps' in blue, or your three fastest laps' today for the braking marker, as the legend under the map says; the points are medians placed on the best lap's line; a minimum speed or a coast has no point, and an improvement to keep has none either), the mean combined G through the corner (braking, cornering and acceleration together, from the recording's longitudinal and lateral acceleration) on this session's laps against the faster laps, and the highest of it on this session's laps against the highest of the day's laps so far at that corner (slow laps left out), when every lap compared has both accelerations (a RaceChrono RCZ import has no vehicle longitudinal and lateral acceleration, so it shows none); it says how hard the car was worked there, not how much grip was left; and a support score from 0 to 0.9. The score says how well the laps back the pattern; it is not a probability.

There are at most three items: at most two changes, each in a different corner, and one thing to keep. The main focus is a change when there is one; an improvement to keep comes last. Changes are ordered by the time your laps lost against the laps compared: through the corner, plus the straight right after it, which is counted with the corner. The card shows that straight as Straight after it. It is an observed time, not proof that the corner caused it: traffic or braking for the next corner shows there too. A corner with no straight right after it, or where a lap compared has no time for that straight, is ranked on its own time. Times lost are compared in steps of 0.1 s; within the same step, the change seen on more of the session's laps comes first. Work on one thing at a time. The suggestions look for an early lift, too much coasting, a low minimum speed and a late throttle return, using the rules of the earlier DrivingCoach app on this app's own laps and corners. Coasting means neither pedal pressed: a stretch held at light throttle (3% or more) counts as throttle, not coasting. They suggest an opportunity, not a promised gain. A change is only suggested when it was seen on at least three laps of the day so far, earlier sessions included, and the session coached still shows it on at least half of its laps compared there (laps with a faster lap to compare with). A pattern you have already left behind is not suggested again; when there is nothing else to suggest, the card then says that patterns seen earlier today do not repeat on most of this session's laps. The measured values are the session coached's own. A lap more than 5% slower than its session's typical (median) lap, in a session of at least three laps, is not read for a pattern: traffic, a warm-up or a cool-down lap says little about technique. The card lists those laps under Left out as much slower than their session's typical lap.

The coach also looks for a throttle picked up too early, a check of this app's own. Pick up the throttle once appears when, after the braking ends and before the slow point, the throttle is held at 20% or more for at least 0.2 s with the brake under 10% and is then released again (to 8% or less for at least 0.2 s and 3 m), while the faster laps compared pick it up once, after the slow point. It shows where the throttle was first picked up on this session's laps against where the faster laps pick it up. A pickup the car gains 1.5 m/s (about 5 km/h) or more on before the release is a drive out of an earlier apex in a series of corners, not a stab, and does not count; neither does a pickup after the car has already slowed to an earlier minimum and gained 1.5 m/s, or one ended by braking again (10% or more). It needs the brake and throttle recorded, with the braking measured from the brake pressure and no gap in the data, and like the other changes it must be seen on at least three laps of the day.

The coach also looks at where you start braking, a check of this app's own. Brake at the same point every lap appears when at least two laps of the session start braking for a corner 10 m or more from where the session usually brakes there, the earliest and the latest are at least 20 m apart (and four sample spacings), and that is at least 20 m more than across your three fastest laps of the day. It reads only laps where the brake is recorded and the braking for the corner starts cleanly (not already under way, not next to a gap in the data) and lasts at least half a second, so a dab does not count. It needs at least three such laps in the session, leaves out much slower laps, and suggests one braking marker at a time.

Speeds carry the unit your recordings declare, without a unit when they declare none, and are never converted. They follow the speed unit setting for recordings that declare no unit. The coach compares speeds in their own units, so a day recorded in mph is coached in mph. If the recordings use different units, the coach still compares them correctly but leaves its speeds out (shown as —) and the card says why.

When there is nothing to suggest, the card says why: for example that a pattern was seen on fewer than three laps today, that patterns seen earlier today do not repeat in this session, that no lap of the session has a faster lap to compare with, or that throttle and brake are not recorded (lift, coasting and throttle return then cannot be compared). The Where to look next card under it lists observations: places worth a closer look, without telling you what to do.

If the suggestions could not be prepared, the card says why; Calculate again tries once more.

Compared laps (groups)

Laps are only ranked against laps driven on the same layout, in the same direction, with the same start/finish line. The app forms these groups by itself. Under the best lap a line such as Group 1 · Detected route · Counterclockwise · 15 of 15 laps ranked says which group the page shows.

When the day has more than one group (for example two layouts), a Compared laps menu lets you pick one. The whole page then follows that group.

Last time here

When your library holds an earlier day on the same track, the Overview ends its analysis cards with Last time here: the date and name of the previous visit, and its Best lap and Theoretical best next to today's, with the difference. The difference is today minus last time, so a negative one (green) is faster today and a positive one (red) slower. The previous visit in the same car is used; if that car never drove the track, the previous visit in any car, and the card names that car. Conditions may have been different, so read the difference as an observation, not a verdict. A time shown as — means that day has none: no ranked lap, or no theoretical best. Below them, Time lost per corner lists each of the track's corners measured on both days, by the name it has in your library: how far that day's laps were from its fastest time through the corner (each session's median, averaged over the day's sessions by their laps), last time and today, with the difference. A smaller time means a more even day in that corner, not a faster corner, because each day is measured against its own best there; where a corner starts and ends can also differ a little between days. A corner measured on only one of the days is not listed. When no corner was measured on both (an older day, or today's corners still being worked out), the card says so. Between the times and the corners, Weather shows the weather of each visit in two lines, for example Then (Session 3, best lap): 19–22 °C, overcast, no rain, wind SW 12 km/h and Today (Session 2, best lap): 14 °C, rain, 1.2 mm of rain, wind W 20 km/h. Each visit is represented by the session that set its best lap. When that session has no weather, the first session with weather is used instead and the line says so, for example Then (Session 1; the best-lap session had none): …. When the previous visit has no weather, its line reads Then: — with the reason: weather lookup was off, its recordings have no time or position, or the day was added to your library before the library kept weather (opening that day once brings it in, if its file has the weather). When today has none yet, its line says why: the weather is still being looked up, weather lookup is off in settings, the weather service could not be reached or had no data, the recording has no time or GPS position, the day has unsaved changes (the weather reaches the library when the day is saved), or it was saved by a newer version of the app. Weather saved by a newer version that this version does not recognise reads conditions this version does not know. Like the rest of the weather, it is a model's values for the area, not measured at the track, and the card credits Open-Meteo.com when either line shows weather. The card only shows weather your library already keeps; it never looks weather up. A first visit, a day whose track was not recognised, or a day saved outside the library shows no card. Today's figures follow the library, so they update a moment after a session is added. For how often each place in Where to look next cost time on all your earlier visits, see its Earlier visits here line.

Best lap of each session

One row per session with its best ranked lap, for example LAP 2 · 5 of 7 laps ranked · typical 1:50.123. The best lap of the day's time is in bold purple. Tap a row to open that lap.

Session details

One row per session with its conditions, setup changes, setup and notes, or No conditions, setup changes or notes, and its weather when the app has it. Tap a row to open Details of Session N:

  • Name: the session's name, up to 160 characters, for example Warm-up. It cannot be blank. The new name is used everywhere: the lap list (Warm-up · LAP 2), the results, the progression and the day report.
  • Conditions, for example Dry, 18 °C.
  • Setup changes, for example Tyres +0.1 bar.
  • Setup: the setup you drove the session with, every field optional.
    • Tyre pressures: choose bar or psi, then fill the table: one column per wheel (FL, FR, RL, RR: front left, front right, rear left, rear right), the Cold row for the pressures set before the session and the Hot row for those measured after it. Leave a wheel empty if you did not measure it. The unit is the previous session's, or bar for the first.
    • Numbers take a decimal point and at most two decimals, for example 2.15. A comma typed on a Polish keyboard becomes a point as you type.
    • Pressures are kept exactly as you entered them, in the unit you chose. Switching between bar and psi never converts the numbers: it only checks them again against the unit's range (0.5–6 bar, 7–90 psi). A pressure outside the range is marked red, the range is said under the table, and Save waits until it is fixed.
    • Tyres: the tyre's name or compound, up to 160 characters, for example Pirelli SC2.
    • Fuel at start (l): the fuel in the tank at the start, 0–200 litres.
    • Same as Session N copies the setup of the session recorded just before this one into the fields, unit included, when that session has a setup. Nothing is kept until you tap Save, so you can change a pressure or two first.
    The session's row then shows it on one line, for example Setup: Cold 2.1 / 2.1 / 2 / 2 bar · Hot 2.4 / 2.4 / 2.3 / — bar · Tyres Pirelli SC2 · Fuel 8.5 l; a wheel without a pressure shows —. A setup saved by a newer version of the app is shown as it is and cannot be edited here.
  • Notes: anything else about the session.

Save keeps the change and the day then has unsaved changes; save the day to write it to the file. Each text can be up to 4096 characters; a text you clear is saved as empty. FlappedEar Overlays reads and edits the same name, conditions, setup changes and notes; it keeps the setup in the file without showing it (see Working with FlappedEar Overlays).

Below the fields, Weather shows the session's weather (see Weather), or why there is none.

Weather

For each session with a recording time and a GPS position, the app looks up the weather once, in the background, from Open-Meteo.com, a free weather service. Nothing is waited for: the results show at once, and the weather appears when it arrives. Each session then shows a line such as Weather: 18 °C, overcast, no rain, wind SW 12 km/h in Session details, in Progression and under each lap in Compare, so you can see whether a quicker session also had different weather.

  • What it is. A weather model's hourly values for the area around the track (about a 1–10 km grid), at the session's time. It is not measured at the track: there is no track temperature, and no rain does not mean the track was dry (it may still be wet from earlier rain).
  • What is shown. The air temperature (a range such as 19–22 °C when it changed by a degree or more during the session), the worst weather of the hours nearest the session (clear, partly cloudy, overcast, fog, drizzle, rain, showers, snow or thunderstorm), the rain in those hours in mm, and the wind (direction it comes from and speed at the middle of the session). Session details adds the strongest gust, humidity, cloud cover and air pressure.
  • What leaves your device. Only the session's first GPS position, rounded to 0.01° (about 1 km), and its date, sent to Open-Meteo.com over an encrypted connection. Nothing else from your recordings is sent, and no account or key is used.
  • Where it is kept. In the day's file, with the session, so a day opened later or offline shows it without asking again. A session whose recording you replace gets its weather looked up again. FlappedEar Overlays keeps it in the file unchanged. For a day in your library, a summary of each session's weather (temperature, weather, rain and wind) is also kept in the library as it arrives (on a day with unsaved changes, when the day is saved), for Last time here on later visits. A session whose recording you replace drops the old weather there too.
  • Recent sessions. For the last few days the service fills in its history from forecasts, and replaces them with its final values within about five days. Weather looked up sooner than five days after a session is shown, and looked up once more when the day is opened after that; the final values are then written with your next save.
  • Offline. Session details says Not available and offers Try again. The app also asks again by itself when the next session is added to the day (at the track the signal may be back by then) and the next time the day opens.
  • Turning it off. Open Settings (the gear in the toolbar; on the day page on a narrow screen, such as a phone, in the ⋮ menu) and turn off Look up the weather for each session. Weather already in a day stays shown.

When a session added to a saved day gets its weather, the day is saved again on its own, as the addition was. For a day you open, the weather is written with your next save and does not make the day unsaved.

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence; the app shows this credit next to the weather.

Circuits

One row per session says which circuit and direction it was matched to, and whether that was inferred from GPS or set by you. Tap the pencil to open Circuit of Session N:

  • Layout name: give the circuit a name, for example Jastrząb full circuit.
  • Clockwise / Counterclockwise: set the direction. On a phone the two buttons are stacked.
  • Also for the sessions on the same route: apply it to the other sessions on that route (on by default).
  • Save regroups the day. Use the detected route removes your setting again.

Use this when a session was put in the wrong group, or its direction is unknown.

VBO and RCZ of one session

When a session has both a VBO and an RCZ of the same drive, the app lines up the two recordings' clocks by their speed traces and combines them by itself, with nothing to confirm. The results show from the VBO straight away; meanwhile the Circuits row says Lining up with its RCZ…, and the combined channels appear when that is done (a few seconds per session):

  • A channel only the RCZ recorded is added to the session. The Circuits row then says, for example, Combined with its RCZ: 1 channel added, and wherever that channel is listed or charted it says from RCZ.
  • A channel both recorded stays the VBO's. Channels the two declare with different units are never combined. VBO channels declare no unit, so a VBO channel is checked against the RCZ's only when their values agree; when they do not, it is never combined.
  • When the two disagree on a channel, the row says so, for example Satellites: the VBO and the RCZ disagree, with three buttons: Keep VBO (the default), Fill gaps (the VBO, and the RCZ where the VBO has no data) and Use RCZ (the RCZ, and the VBO where the RCZ has no data).
  • When the RCZ was lined up but has nothing to add and agrees with the VBO, the row says so quietly, for example Lined up with its RCZ (−0.14 s); nothing to add.
  • If lining up an RCZ you just added fails, the app says RCZ added to Session 2, but it could not be combined; it is kept and tried again when the day opens. and the row says Not combined with its RCZ: lining them up failed.
  • When the clocks cannot be lined up, nothing is combined and the row says why in one line, for example Not combined with its RCZ: their speed traces do not line up clearly.

Laps, lap times and the ranking always come from the session's primary recording alone (the VBO, unless you make the RCZ primary), as does the theoretical best; the combined channels are used by lap charts, comparisons and the Car and Driver cards. Otherwise a session whose RCZ added nothing and agrees with its VBO looks as before.

When a saved day's RCZ is not where the day says, or the file there is another recording, a card lists it (The RCZ of 1 session could not be used) with Find recordings in a folder…; the session uses its VBO alone until then.

Checking the clock

Under a session with both recordings, Check clock lines up the two clocks again and shows what was measured, before anything changes (the row says Comparing the clocks of the VBO and the RCZ… meanwhile; Cancel next to it stops the check and nothing changes):

  • whether the clocks line up (The clocks line up.), or why not, for example The clocks cannot be lined up: their speed traces do not line up clearly.;
  • the measured offset, for example Measured from the speed traces: VBO time = RCZ time +0.10 ± 0.02 s, and the clock drift when one can be measured;
  • how well the speed traces match over the time both recorded, and what the loggers' own start times say.

Then choose:

  • Accept and combine (only when the clocks line up, and not while recordings are being added) combines the RCZ with the measured offset. Your choices for channels the two disagree on are kept.
  • Refuse keeps the RCZ beside the session without combining it: the session's charts and cards use its primary recording only, and the row says Its RCZ is kept beside it and not combined until the day is opened again.

Don't combine, under a combined session, refuses straight away, without a new check.

For a session whose primary recording is its VBO, a refusal lasts until the day is opened again. The day file, which FlappedEar Overlays reads too, saves a refused RCZ as Overlays saves a removed fusion: as a recording kept with the session and not combined. It has no place to record that you refused it, and the app combines a VBO session's RCZ by itself, so when the day opens again it lines up that RCZ and combines it again. Check the clock again and refuse it again if you need to. A session whose primary is its RCZ (see Choosing the primary recording) keeps its VBO apart when the day is opened again.

Choosing the primary recording

A session's primary recording gives its laps, lap times and ranking. It is the VBO when both were imported. Make RCZ primary under the session makes the RCZ its primary recording instead, as Overlays' Run details does (Make VBO primary switches back):

  • While the RCZ is read, the row says Reading the RCZ as this session's recording…. The day's laps, groups and ranking are then worked out again with the session's laps from the RCZ, and every result is calculated again from them. Cancel next to that row stops the change, and the primary recording stays as it was.
  • The other recording is kept beside the session and not combined (Its VBO is kept beside it and not combined). The day keeps it this way when it is opened again.
  • A circuit layout you set for the session is not carried over: the new recording starts with its detected route. Laps you excluded and the laps you compared in the old recording do not apply to the new one. They stay in the day file and apply again when you switch back, as long as that recording's laps are worked out the same way: a circuit layout you had set for it is not restored, so after switching back with a layout lost, they no longer apply.

The primary recording changes only on a saved day: with unsaved changes the row says Save the day before changing the primary recording. The day then has changes until you save it. Before the switch, the app checks that the other recording's file is still where it was read from and holds the same recording; if not, nothing changes and the row says so, for example The RCZ file has changed since it was read. Open the day again, then make it primary.

While a clock is checked or a primary recording is changed, Add recordings, Find recordings, Retry recordings and leaving the day wait for it: the app says Wait until the session's recordings are checked or changed.

Notes

At the bottom, Notes lists messages from the analysis, each starting with the session it is about. Sessions whose circuit could not be identified are listed under their own heading; their laps are not compared. Tap one to set its circuit.

Missing recordings

A saved day only points to its recording files. If one was moved or deleted, the day still opens, and a red card at the top lists the sessions that could not be opened. See Find recordings in a folder.
