A lap

Open any lap from the lap list, a session's best lap, the Best lap of the day bar or the best lap's map. The page shows the lap's time, its map and its channels.

The summary

  • An amber bar with the lap's time, labelled Best lap of the day, Best lap of Session N or This lap.
  • For any other timed lap, a blue Best lap of the day bar under it with the best lap of the day and its time (on a day with more than one track layout, the best on this lap's layout), and the gap, for example +0.412 s to the best of the day (red when this lap is slower). When the two laps can be compared, tap the blue bar to compare this lap (A, amber) with the best lap of the day (B, blue).
  • A red line for each reason the lap is not ranked.
  • Compare with…, and Exclude from ranking… or Include in ranking.

Excluding a lap

A lap with traffic or a yellow flag can spoil the best lap, the theoretical best and the typical lap. Tap Exclude from ranking…, enter a reason and tap Exclude.

The lap stays in the list as Excluded: <reason>. Include in ranking brings it back. Exclusions are saved with the day.

The map

  • The trace is coloured by speed, purple (slow) to yellow (fast); the bar under the map gives the range in km/h.
  • The start/finish line is black with a white edge (white on a map with no background), with an arrow for the direction.
  • A white dot shows where the cursor is (see Charts).
  • Show the best lap (…) in grey draws the best lap of the day under this one. On street, satellite and Apple Maps backgrounds it is drawn in a light, see-through line.
  • Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag to move (on a phone or tablet, use two fingers; one finger scrolls the page where the page scrolls). North is up.

Map background

The layers button (top right of the map) chooses the background for every map in the app until you close it:

  • Streets: OpenStreetMap.
  • Satellite: MapTiler imagery, only in builds that have a MapTiler key. It is the default when available.
  • Apple Maps: Apple's map, only on iPhone, iPad and Mac. The trace, the start/finish line, the dots and the cursor are drawn on it as on the other backgrounds, and it moves the same way, but it zooms in a little less far than the other backgrounds. Legal (top left of the map) opens Apple's legal notices for the map.

Map tiles need a network connection the first time a track is shown; tiles you have seen are kept for later. Apple Maps also needs a network connection and keeps what it has loaded itself.

Charts

Under the map, or beside it on a wide screen, Channels charts the lap over time. By default: speed, lateral G, longitudinal G, and throttle or brake when recorded.

  • Tap a chart or drag sideways across it to move the cursor. The value at the cursor is shown top right of each chart, and the white dot on the map follows.
  • The slider moves the cursor across the whole lap.
  • Zoom in around the cursor, Zoom out, or show the Whole lap. The range shown is written on the right.
  • Longitudinal G is drawn with braking upward.
  • Add any recorded channel with Add a channel (up to four charts); remove one with its ✕. Your choice is kept for other laps until you close the app. A channel that came from the session's RCZ says from RCZ in the menu and under its chart (see VBO and RCZ of one session).
  • A gap in the recording stays a gap. A chart says No data in this range or why a channel is not available.

Coasting

Coasting is time at speed with neither pedal pressed. The card gives the total, for example 4.2 s · 128 m over 9 episodes (5.1 % of the lap), says whether it was measured from the brake and accelerator pedals or inferred from longitudinal G, lists it by segment, and lists each episode: select one to move the cursor there.

Coasting is not a mistake by itself: a lift can settle the car or be forced by traffic.
