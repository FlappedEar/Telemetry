Your profile and skills

Profile, in the bar at the bottom or the rail at the side, adds up every day in your library: how much you have driven, your records at each track, each track day by day, and your skills. It appears once the app keeps a library.

All days

Days, sessions, timed laps, distance, time on track (time moving), tracks and cars, over every day in your library. Distance and time come from each session's recording. A day saved before this version has none until you open it again; until then a line says how many sessions the distance and time cover, and with none measured they show —.

Skills

The skills follow DrivingCoach's 12-skill model, grouped as Braking, Corner, Exit and Lap. Each skill the app measures has a level from 1 to 5, worked out over your last 3 days that measured it. The bands are fixed by what a fast, experienced driver repeats lap after lap, not by your own day, so level 5 means at the limit. A level also needs the laps to show it: level 5 needs 15 ranked laps; with 5 to 14 the level is at most 4, and with fewer at most 3. The trend compares the bands only, so more laps alone is no change. Beside the level:

  • Confidence, from the ranked laps measured: low below 5, medium from 5 to 14, high from 15.
  • How many ranked laps over how many days it comes from, and whether it is improving, steady or declining against the days before.
  • The measured value the level comes from.

A spread needs at least 3 laps at a corner. A skill with nothing measured yet, for example because the recording has no throttle or brake, says Needs more evidence. The levels are against fixed bands, not a score, and like the coach they point at an opportunity, never a promised gain.

The same skills appear under Coach, below the Next session card.

Cars and tracks

Cars lists each car you have driven with its days, distance and time on track. Tracks lists each track with its direction and visits (a new track takes the name of its circuit, or Track N where none is known), and your records there: the best lap, the best theoretical lap and the best typical lap (the median of a session), each with its date and session. When you have been to a track more than once, a line says how much faster or slower the best lap of your last visit was than the visit before.

Track notebook

Each track has a notebook for the next visit. Open it with the book button next to the track in the library, or from the ⋮ menu of a day at that track, as Track notebook. The notebook is kept with the track in your profile, so every day at that track shows the same notebook. It is saved as you type.

  • To try: things to try next time. Type one and tap +. Tick it when it is done; it moves under Done. The bin removes it. A notebook holds up to 256 things to try.
  • Notes: anything about the track, such as grip, bumps or where to brake.
  • Corners: one note for each of the track's corners. A corner measured while the notebook is open is added to the list.

When you import a profile from another device (see Moving your library to another device), the notebooks of the same track are combined, whether or not a day is added: notes that differ are added after yours, a note on a corner this device does not know joins the general notes under the corner's name, and things to try are added unless they are already there. Nothing of yours is replaced. If something from the file does not fit (a notebook holds at most 256 things to try, and each note has a length limit), it is left out and the import says so.

Day by day

Day by day lists each track in each car on its own, one group of lines per day, oldest first (the last 10 days when there are more); undated days come last and are not part of a trend or a new best. Lap times, speeds and spreads depend on the track and the car, so a day is only compared with days at the same track in the same car. For each day:

  • The day's best lap, marked new best here in this car when it was faster than every earlier day there in that car (equal is not), and the typical lap (the sessions' medians, weighted by their ranked laps).
  • Four figures, the same ones the skills measure: Braking-point spread, Minimum speed below your best (against your best ever at each corner there in that car, so a new best there changes the earlier days' figures too), Off the throttle to braking (lifting and coasting before braking; needs the throttle and brake recorded) and Lap time spread. Lower is better for each. Each is the median over the corners the day measured, so days may differ in the corners behind it. A figure the day did not measure is left out, never shown as zero; a day with none of them says so.
  • The weather kept for its sessions: the weather model's conditions and the most rain it gave over one session, with how many of the day's sessions it covers when not all of them, or that no weather was kept.

A trend needs at least 3 dated days at the same track in the same car. With fewer, a line says how many there are and that a trend needs at least 3; the days are still listed. From 3 dated days on, First day to last shows each figure on the first and the last day that measured it, with their dates and how many days measured it. A figure needs to be measured on at least 3 days itself: one measured on fewer (no pedals recorded on some days, say) says on how many days it was measured instead.

Whether the track was wet or dry is not recorded. The weather comes from a weather model for the area at each session's time, not from the track surface, so days are not split into wet and dry; the card says so.

Corners that keep costing time

A corner is listed when it was among the three corners that cost the most time (0.1 s or more) on two or more visits to a track. Each shows the time lost on average and on how many visits, and how it stands now: Still costing time when it lost time on one of the last two visits that measured it, Fading when the last two visits since it last lost time did not measure it (no lap was read there), and Not lost on the last two visits when both measured it without losing time. With one visit to a track the list is empty and says so.
