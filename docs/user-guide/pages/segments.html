Editing segments

Segments are the corners and straights the analysis uses. The app proposes them from your best lap; you can rename, move, split, merge or remove them.

Opening the editor

On the day page, in the Theoretical best card, tap Edit segments.

What you see

  • The best lap's map with the segment boundaries as dots, beside the list when the window is wider than it is tall and at least 600 points wide (for example a phone held sideways). The selected segment is blue, with its boundaries as white dots; changes you have not applied yet show as purple dots.
  • Automatic segments or Edited segments, the theoretical best and the number of segments.
  • Review proposals, which opens the optional review of the automatic proposals.
  • The segments in lap order: short name (C1, S1…), name, type, start and end in metres from the line, length, and fastest time.

Changing a segment

Tap a segment to select it. Then:

  • Name and type (Corner, Straight, Sector).
  • Start and End: nudge by −10, −1, +1 or +10 m, or tap Pick on map under one and then tap the track line on the map (see Placing a boundary on the map). Move the neighbouring segment too keeps the next segment attached (on by default). Then Apply, or Reset.
  • Split here: set the point with the slider, or with Pick on map.
  • Merge with the next segment.
  • Remove.

Every change times all laps again. Undo and Redo are in the toolbar.

Placing a boundary on the map

  1. Tap Pick on map under Start, End or the split slider. The map says Tap the track line to place the start (or the end, or the split), and the button reads Tap the map… (cancel); tap it again to stop.
  2. Tap or click the lap's line where the boundary goes. The nearest point of the best lap is used, to 0.1 m, and shows as a purple dot.
  3. Tap Apply (or Split here). Like any change, it can be undone.

A tap more than about 3% of the track's size away from the line says Tap on the lap's track line. Where another part of the track passes close by, such as a crossing or a parallel straight, the app cannot tell which one you meant and says Another part of the track passes close by here. Set the distance with the buttons instead. A split point must lie inside the segment. These are the same rules as in FlappedEar Overlays. On a phone, one finger taps and two fingers still zoom and move the map.

Reviewing the proposals

Segments are approved automatically, so you never have to review them: the results, and the next session you add, use them straight away. They stay the same when you add a session, even one with a new best lap, so a corner keeps its name and place all day; the coach relies on that to follow a corner from session to session. One exception: when the new best lap took a line the segments cannot follow, so some of its segments get no time, the segments are measured again on that lap. Names you gave them carry over to the new segment covering the same part of the lap, and the day keeps them like segments you approved. Segments you moved, split or merged are yours and stay as they are. In the editor, Review proposals shows the corners and straights the app proposed from the lap the segments are measured on (normally the best lap), as FlappedEar Overlays does. For each proposal you see:

  • Its state: Approved (it is one of the segments), Proposed (open, for example after you removed that segment), Rejected, or Overlaps approved (a segment you edited covers part of it).
  • Its name and type; for a corner, how far it turns and which way, for example Corner · 92° left.
  • Its start and end in metres from the line, each with its tolerance (±), and its length. Crosses start/finish marks one that runs across the line.
  • Uncertainty notes for a start or end that is less clear: Short straight, Corners connect without a straight or Near a GPS gap in this lap.
  • For a corner, its Geometric apex, where the track's shape turns most (not where you were slowest), or why there is none, such as Multiple apexes — review manually.

What you can do:

  • Reject an open proposal, and Restore it again. A rejection does not change the segments; it is saved with the day, as FlappedEar Overlays saves it.
  • Approve all makes every open proposal a segment, whatever its uncertainty; rejected ones stay out. Every lap is timed again.
  • Recompute works out the proposals again from the lap.

Every change can be undone with Undo and Redo in the toolbar, here or in the editor: they share one history.

After Restore automatic in the editor, a proposal you rejected is approved again with the others; its rejection shows again if you later remove that segment.

Keeping your segments

Save the day to keep the segments. Your corrections are saved with the day and are never replaced by automatic segments. Restore automatic goes back to the proposed segments after asking.
